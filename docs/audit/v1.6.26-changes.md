## The fix

**What was wrong** (measured since v1.6.20). v1.6.8 made a wallet's referrer a property of the
wallet, written by the first 64-byte `hookData` whose wallet word equalled `tx.origin`. The subject
was the one party calldata cannot forge; the authorisation was a comparison with it. Under ERC-4337,
and for every 7702 sponsor and relayer, `tx.origin` is the party that SUBMITS the transaction while
somebody else writes its calldata. One user operation carrying `abi.encode(attacker, bundler)`
therefore bound the bundler to the attacker for life, and every later trade the bundler relayed paid
the attacker the referrer share and credited the attacker's `referred` track in the fee ledger.

**The ruling** (owner, 2026-10-04): a binding is authenticated, never inferred. A wallet is bound
only by its own call or by its own signature. The chain cannot tell a bundler's key from a user's, so
there is no "only for the risky cases" switch: no trade binds anybody.

**The registry** (`src/FeastReferrals.sol`, new). Immutable and admin-less: no owner, no setter, no
clear, no upgrade path.

- `bind(referrer)` binds `msg.sender`. An EOA sends it; an ERC-4337 account batches it into a user
  operation and binds itself.
- `bindWithSig(wallet, referrer, deadline, signature)` binds `wallet` on its own EIP-712 signature,
  relayed by anyone, checked with OpenZeppelin's `SignatureChecker.isValidSignatureNow`: ECDSA for a
  wallet without code, ERC-1271 for one with code. Domain `FeastReferrals` / `1` / chain id /
  registry; struct `Bind(address wallet,address referrer,uint256 deadline)`. The separator is
  computed per call, so it follows the chain id through a fork.
- No nonce: a wallet binds once, so a replayed signature reverts `AlreadyBound`, and
  `block.timestamp > deadline` is `SignatureExpired`.
- The refusals are the hook's own since v1.6.8, through one `_bind` for both entries: `ZeroReferrer`,
  `SelfReferral`, `BurnReferrer`, `AlreadyBound`; plus `BadSignature`. The signature is checked first,
  so a relayed call learns nothing about a wallet that did not sign it.
- Event `ReferrerBound(address indexed wallet, address indexed referrer)`.

The ABI is frozen exactly as the brief gave it; the app and keeper lanes are briefed against it. The
EIP-712 vectors — type hashes, a domain separator, a struct hash, a digest and a signature — are in
`docs/interface.md`. They were computed twice outside Solidity: by hand with `cast keccak` and
`cast abi-encode`, and by alloy's encoder through `cast wallet sign --data`, whose signature over the
typed data equals the one over the hand-built digest. `test_theEip712VectorsTheAppSignsAgainst` pins
them, and the registry accepting that signature is what shows its digest is the same.

**The hook** (`src/FeastHook.sol`).

- The constructor takes `referrals_`, after `guardian_`, kept as `FeastReferrals public immutable
  referrals` (`:71`, `:88`, `:95`).
- The in-hook binding is gone: the `_referrerOf` mapping, its write in the 64-byte branch, and the
  hook's `ReferrerBound` event. So, by the ruling below, is the hook's `referrerOf` view.
- `_referrerFor`, after the protocol-initiated short-circuit (unchanged), reads
  `referrals.referrerOf(tx.origin)` (`:672-698`). A bound wallet's referrer is paid and `hookData` is
  not consulted.
- Unbound, both forms pay the referrer they name for this trade only, with the refusals each had
  (`:700-730`). In either form the payer itself is paid nothing (v1.6 ruling 5, Q5). In the 64-byte
  form so are the burn address and the wallet the trade names.
- `_referrerFor` is now `view`.

## What did not fit: every byte decision

EIP-170's limit is 24,576. `FeastHook` was 24,566 at v1.6.25. Every row below is
`forge build --sizes src/FeastHook.sol` on a copy of `src/`, which reproduces v1.6.25's 24,566 before
any change.

| build | runtime | margin |
|---|---|---|
| v1.6.25 (`bf1d930`) | 24,566 | 10 |
| the brief's shape, literal: the view kept, `tx.origin` and the wallet word read, three Solidity call sites | 24,887 | −311 |
| the same, the three reads sharing one Solidity helper | 24,750 | −174 |
| **relaxation 1**, the view dropped: Solidity helper | 24,686 | −110 |
| relaxation 1, the read in assembly | 24,617 | −41 |
| relaxation 1, assembly, and the self-referral check on the wallet path folded into the per-trade refusals | 24,596 | −20 |
| **relaxation 2**, the wallet-word lookup dropped: one Solidity call | 24,602 | −26 |
| **relaxation 2, the read in assembly with the decoder's checks — shipped** | **24,541** | **35** |

**The coordinator's ruling** (2026-10-04, after the 24,750 measurement) authorised two relaxations,
in order, each only if the build before it was still over, and nothing else beyond semantic-free
shrinking:

1. **The hook's `referrerOf(address)` view is dropped** (64 bytes on the helper build). The app lane
   is briefed to read `FeastReferrals.referrerOf`. Still over at every spelling of the read: the best,
   24,596, was 20 over.
2. **The 64-byte wallet-word registry lookup is dropped.** Lifetime binding applies through
   `tx.origin` only, and the 64-byte form behaves like the 32-byte form: per trade, never binds.
   **A smart account's lifetime binding is honoured only on its own direct trades, and this is a
   bytecode decision, not a design one.** An ERC-4337 account is never `tx.origin`, so on its bundled
   trades its binding is recorded but not read. What pays is the referrer the trade names; an
   integrator that wants the account's referrer paid reads `referrerOf(account)` and names it per
   trade. `test_aSmartAccountsBindingIsNotReadOnATradeABundlerSubmits` pins the boundary.

**Then, without semantic change: the read is the Solidity call spelled in assembly** (`:689-697`),
because the Solidity call left the hook 26 over. It is the call Solidity makes:

- `staticcall` of `referrerOf(address)` (`0xd21cacdf`, pinned against `referrals.referrerOf.selector`
  by `test_theHookReadsTheRegistryItWasBuiltWith`) with `tx.origin`, using scratch memory only;
- refused unless the call succeeded, returned at least a word, and that word is a clean address —
  the decoder's own three checks (the third, the clean address, costs 6 of the margin's bytes);
- a failure is refused with empty data rather than the callee's, which a mapping getter has none of
  to give.

Four tests pin each refusal against a registry that would otherwise be paid as if it answered: no
code, a short answer, a dirty word, a revert. Spelling the selector as `referrals.referrerOf.selector`
rather than the literal measured 24,568 (margin 8) and was not taken.

**Measured, not shipped: a registry-side resolver.** A view `referrerFor(origin, wallet)` on
`FeastReferrals` would return the origin's binding, else the wallet's unless it is the origin. With
the hook making that one call in assembly it measured **24,545, margin 31**, and it would keep the
smart-account path. It adds a function to the registry's frozen ABI and is not one of the two
authorised relaxations, so it is not in this release; it is the owner's to choose.

**Not taken: anything outside the referral path.** No other function in the hook moved a byte to make
room. Removing the mapping moves `_tipFloorOf` from slot 4 to slot 3. No test reads that slot, and
the slots of `_pools`, `_referrerPending` and `_purchased` (0, 1, 2) do not move.
