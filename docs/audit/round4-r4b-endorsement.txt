**HIGH**

**H1 — One donated input-token unit permanently blocks that token’s conversions.**

The new refusal computes consumption by subtracting the converter’s **entire ending input-token balance**, without accounting for its starting balance. [src/TorchConverter.sol:174](src/TorchConverter.sol:174)

An attacker transfers one unit of ordinary quote token Q to the converter. A subsequent fully filled request for 100 Q leaves that donated unit behind, reports consumption of 99, and reverts. Larger donations can instead produce arithmetic underflow.

The donation survives every reverted attempt. Smaller requests and different routes cannot remove it: each hop spends only that call’s offered amount, and there is no donation-recovery path. Every platform bucket using Q becomes unconvertible indefinitely. Creator/referrer BNB conversion also fails, although their direct quote claim remains available.

This materially worsens round 3’s pre-existing-balance defect: **any positive donation now suffices**. Measure the current execution’s consumed input independently of unsolicited balances.

**H2 — The withdrawn remainder still exists at later hops and the platform purchase.**

Two successful execution paths retain unowned assets:

1. **Later converter hop.** The loop does not verify each hop’s consumed input. Only the original token is checked afterward. A fully consumed Q→X hop followed by an X→WBNB hop consuming 98% can satisfy the route’s 97% output floor while leaving 2% of X permanently in the converter. The claimant or platform bucket has already paid for all of it. [src/TorchConverter.sol:161](src/TorchConverter.sol:161), [src/TorchConverter.sol:182](src/TorchConverter.sol:182)
2. **Platform purchase.** `platformBuyBack` subtracts the requested `amountIn` from settled holdings. Its callback transfers only the actual venue debt and returns only purchased coin. Positive partial output therefore succeeds while the unspent WBNB loses its ledger entry. [src/TorchHook.sol:909](src/TorchHook.sol:909), [src/TorchHook.sol:627](src/TorchHook.sol:627)

For example, settled holdings of 1,000, a permitted request of 200, and actual consumption of 20 leave 980 WBNB physically held but only 800 recorded.

The withdrawal regression exercises a single hop consuming just 10%; the value bound independently rejects that execution. It does not hold either missing boundary. [test/TorchPlatformButton.t.sol:321](test/TorchPlatformButton.t.sol:321)

Enforce full consumption at **each** withdrawn settlement boundary and revert atomically. This finding does not request remainder reconciliation.

**H3 — The route floor still accepts economically destructive attacker-owned routes.**

Expected output derives entirely from the pools the permissionless caller selects. Each pool is compared with its own history; their product has no independent quote/native valuation. [src/TorchConverter.sol:149](src/TorchConverter.sol:149), [src/TorchConverter.sol:259](src/TorchConverter.sol:259)

An attacker can supply mature Q/X and X/WBNB pools where valuable Q buys X and X buys negligible WBNB. Both spot/TWAP deviations can be zero. With sufficiently small execution impact, the route fills completely and satisfies its own expected-output floor. The attacker’s liquidity receives Q; the protocol receives negligible value.

The hook accepts this route from any caller. [src/TorchHook.sol:867](src/TorchHook.sol:867)

The regression only makes a mock underpay its advertised price. It stays green through an execution faithfully following a bad advertised price. [test/TorchConverterBounds.t.sol:146](test/TorchConverterBounds.t.sol:146)

Protocol spending needs an economic reference independent of the selected route.

**H4 — WBNB-denominated platform buckets still have no accepted spending path.**

The converter rejects an empty route, requires a nonempty route to end in wrapped native, and forbids any hop from revisiting the input token. For wrapped-native input, those requirements are mutually exclusive. [src/TorchConverter.sol:133](src/TorchConverter.sol:133)

`convertPlatformQuote` invokes the converter unconditionally. Consequently, the required platform-coin/WBNB pool accumulates its ordinary 20% platform allocation with no path into `platformNative`. The new purchase entry point cannot help: it spends only holdings already credited there. [src/TorchHook.sol:865](src/TorchHook.sol:865), [src/TorchHook.sol:894](src/TorchHook.sol:894)

Provide and test an already-wrapped-native settlement path. WBNB-denominated `claimAsBnb` also needs this treatment.

**H5 — Positive dust can still suppress productive buybacks indefinitely.**

Both purchase buttons accept any positive purchased amount and then consume the entire hourly interval. [src/TorchHook.sol:817](src/TorchHook.sol:817), [src/TorchHook.sol:908](src/TorchHook.sol:908)

A caller can front-run a productive press with an amount buying only a few raw coin units. The transaction succeeds, possibly with a zero tip, and blocks meaningful spending for an hour. Repeating at each expiry keeps substantial buckets practically unusable.

The zero-output regressions do not exercise positive dust. [test/TorchBuyback.t.sol:189](test/TorchBuyback.t.sol:189), [test/TorchPlatformButton.t.sol:336](test/TorchPlatformButton.t.sol:336)

Require meaningful progress before consuming the interval.

**H6 — Accrue-then-divide can still make an entire funded pot unclaimable.**

At finalisation, the contract still computes:

`index = floor(accrued × 10^27 / totalFees)`

Entitlement then divides by the same precision. [src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:217](src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:217), [src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:233](src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:233)

With one payer, `totalFees = 10^30`, and aggregate funding of 999 raw platform units, the index is zero and the sole payer receives zero. All 999 units remain permanently held. The empty-epoch rollover does not apply because the denominator is positive.

The factory imposes no denomination relationship that excludes this case. Raw-unit amounts also have no guaranteed economic insignificance across arbitrary launch supplies. The claimed dust bound therefore does not hold.

The fuzz test excludes this regime by bounding each payer’s fees at `10^24` and funding at least `10^18`. [test/TorchEpochRewards.t.sol:145](test/TorchEpochRewards.t.sol:145)

Accruing 100 and 900 before division now correctly fixes that particular fragmented-funding example. It does not close the precision class. Use full-precision cumulative pro-rata entitlement or otherwise preserve the amount discarded by index construction.

**M4 — The advertised 3% TWAP protection remains broader than execution protection.**

`0.97 / 1.0001^900 = 0.8865`

of corresponding TWAP-priced output, before pool fees. [src/TorchConverter.sol:209](src/TorchConverter.sol:209), [src/TorchConverter.sol:182](src/TorchConverter.sol:182)

**Completeness**

The mandate is **partly solved. I would not endorse this tree for internal v1.**

| Fee share | Endpoint assessment |
|---|---|
| Self-burn | Normally purchases and genuinely burns the coin, minus its tip. Realised claim debiting is present, but positive-dust suppression remains. |
| Referrer | Direct healthy-token claims reach the recorded referrer. Conversion can strand intermediate value; taxed delivery is unchecked. |
| Creator | Direct claims and fixed-recipient vesting work. Burn-address creator fees redirect to self-buyback. Converted delivery has the same remaining limitations. |
| Platform | The complete lifecycle remains unsound: donation blocking, destructive routes, WBNB input rejection, purchase remainders, dust suppression and reward precision prevent assured disposition. |

No new owner, upgrade or pause path was found in the shipped modules. The restricted governor and callee-enforced band are implemented. The purchase-day epoch rule is also implemented as now instructed; I have not counted that deliberate attribution change as a defect.

Endorsement requires closing the payment failures, restoring the conversion’s operational bounds, making deployment executable, reconciling the normative documents, and adding regressions that fail at the uncovered boundaries.
