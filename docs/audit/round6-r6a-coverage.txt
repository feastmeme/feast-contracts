**HIGH**

**H1 — A nominally sufficient request can still consume the hour while spending dust.**

`buyBackAndBurn` checks `amountIn >= minimum` before swapping. Afterwards it receives `quoteSpent`, correctly debits that amount, but requires only positive coin output before starting the hour. It never compares realised spending with the minimum. [TorchHook.sol:763](src/TorchHook.sol:763), [TorchHook.sol:768](src/TorchHook.sol:768), [TorchHook.sol:775](src/TorchHook.sol:775), [TorchHook.sol:781](src/TorchHook.sol:781)

Concrete construction:

1. Use a coin-as-currency0 pool whose locked position starts at tick 0. Trading through additional liquidity can put the price below that range; the factory permits a creator allocation, and Torch registers no liquidity-addition/removal callbacks restricting other positions. [TorchFactory.sol:129](src/TorchFactory.sol:129), [TorchFactory.sol:135](src/TorchFactory.sol:135), [TorchCLHookBase.sol:36](src/TorchCLHookBase.sol:36)
2. Let pending self-burn attribution be `10^18`, with mature spot and average at tick `-1000`. Leave liquidity `1000` in an attacker-controlled range `[-1200,-800]`, with no other active liquidity before the locked range.
3. Request `10^16`, exactly the minimum. The fixed limit reaches approximately tick `-409`, beyond the small position but before the locked position. The small position supplies approximately ten raw coin units for eleven raw quote units including fee; traversal through the subsequent empty range consumes no meaningful additional input. [TorchHook.sol:505](src/TorchHook.sol:505), [TorchHook.sol:530](src/TorchHook.sol:530)
4. Positive output passes, and the hour starts. Restore deeper liquidity and the prior price afterwards: a productive press is now blocked by the consumed interval. This can repeat at expiry.

The accepted set still includes presses moving an arbitrarily small fraction of the bucket. Refuse insufficient **realised** progress before consuming the clock; preserve atomic rollback and address the resulting shallow-pool liveness boundary explicitly.

**MEDIUM**

**M1 — The dust exemption still leaves spendable buckets trapped by output granularity. Fresh construction of the rationing class.**

The whole-bucket exception depends only on whether pending quote is at most 10,000 raw units. It does not account for the quote needed to purchase one raw coin unit. Above the threshold, every permitted request can produce zero even though the whole bucket would produce positive output. [TorchHook.sol:749](src/TorchHook.sol:749), [TorchHook.sol:781](src/TorchHook.sol:781)

For example, a six-decimal quote and coin-as-currency0 launch can use 1,000 raw coin units, opening tick `138200`, and liquidity approximately `1,001,901`. This consumes the seed allocation and prices one raw coin unit around one million raw quote units. The factory admits this configuration. [TorchFactory.sol:105](src/TorchFactory.sol:105), [TorchFactory.sol:145](src/TorchFactory.sol:145)

After historical trading, let pending be approximately 2,000,000 quote units, with LP fees already collected and price near that opening price:

- Maximum permitted input: 400,000; output rounds to zero.
- Whole-bucket input: 2,000,000; output would be one raw coin unit, comfortably inside the price limit.

With trading stopped, waiting and smaller retries cannot spend the entitlement. Six quote decimals do not constrain coin-unit granularity. The exemption must address executable output, not just quote denomination.

**M2 — CREATE2 still permits substitution of a pending launch’s economics. Known open.**

Another caller can copy a pending launch’s salt, name, symbol and supply, substitute creator, quote, shares, allocation and opening configuration, and deploy first. Those substituted fields do not enter the address commitment. Single-sided creation requires no contributed quote. The intended transaction then fails while its predicted address contains different economics. [TorchFactory.sol:120](src/TorchFactory.sol:120), [TorchFactory.sol:182](src/TorchFactory.sol:182)

Bind the deployment address to authenticated creation intent and configuration.

**M3 — Direct claims accept nominal settlement without verifying delivery. Known open.**

Suppose an upgradeable quote starts taxing outbound vault transfers by 1%. Anyone may trigger a creator claim of 100; accounting retires 100 and the vault transfers nominally 100, but the creator receives 99. No recipient balance check preserves or refuses the missing entitlement. Referrer claims have the same shape. [TorchHook.sol:606](src/TorchHook.sol:606), [TorchHook.sol:617](src/TorchHook.sol:617), [TorchHook.sol:585](src/TorchHook.sol:585)

This remains contrary to the explicit promise of balance-delta protection against quotes becoming fee-on-transfer. [plan.md:226](docs/plan.md:226)

**M4 — The accepted execution set remains wider than the advertised TWAP-relative bound. Known open.**

Self-buyback permits spot to differ by 300 ticks from the average, then applies its execution limit relative to that displaced spot. For a constant-liquidity coin-as-currency0 purchase reaching the `1.03 × sqrtPrice` limit, those allowances can combine to deliver approximately:

`1 / (1.03 × 1.0001^300) = 0.94218`

of TWAP-priced output before fees—a 5.78% shortfall. [TorchHook.sol:505](src/TorchHook.sol:505), [TorchHook.sol:837](src/TorchHook.sol:837)

Likewise, three converter hops can combine adverse 300-tick displacements with the route’s additional 3% allowance, reaching approximately `0.88652` of corresponding TWAP-priced output. [TorchConverter.sol:184](src/TorchConverter.sol:184), [TorchConverter.sol:226](src/TorchConverter.sol:226)

These are execution-bound discrepancies, independent of the removed protocol-routing attack. Define and test the aggregate economic bound promised by R8. [plan.md:427](docs/plan.md:427)
