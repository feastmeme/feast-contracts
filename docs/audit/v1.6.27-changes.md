## The fixes

### `CA-344cf3b11c` — the window's seed burns its own remainder, never the hook's balance

**What was wrong.** Both window seeds, `seedWindowPool` (an auction that cleared) and
`seedWindowOpening` (no participation), read `sent = balanceOf(hook)` after the window's transfer.
`_burnUnplaced` then took the hook's whole FEAST balance after seeding as the unplaced remainder and
reverted `PoolTooShallow` above `sent / 100`. FEAST that reached the hook by any other route counted
as remainder: the team's vesting (anyone may call `release`), an emission claim, a stray transfer.
Since v1.6.24 `settle` has no deadline, so one such transfer before a late settle stranded the
window's 60% of the supply for good. The PoC uses the team's own vesting, 11.4M FEAST fifty days after
the close.

**What changed** (`FeastFactory.sol:969-975`). The remainder is now the seed's own:

- `sent` is what the window delivered. That is `WINDOW_SHARE`, the share genesis handed the window,
  less what the window still holds after its transfer: the tranche it sold, or nothing when nobody
  committed.
- The remainder is `sent` less what the seeding took out of the hook, measured across the seed call.
- FEAST that was already on the hook is not the seed's business. It is neither counted nor burned. It
  stays where its sender put it, owned by nothing, like any token sent to a contract that cannot move
  it.

`WINDOW_SHARE` (`:933-936`) is the same remainder of the same constants as `genesis`'s `poolAmount`.
`genesis` is untouched. The exact-burn tests below would fail if the two ever differed.

**`sent` cannot underflow.** The window keeps at most its tranche, half of its holding. Its holding is
at most the whole supply, so that is at most 50%, short of the 60% genesis handed it.

**The cost, pinned rather than hidden.** FEAST sent straight to the **window** before it settles is
placed with the rest, because the window sizes its position from its whole holding. `sent` does not
count it, so the subtraction saturates at zero instead of going negative. The seed's own rounding
remainder is then left on the hook rather than burned: measured 2,043 wei, and 2,057 with the coin as
currency1. v1.6.26 burned it in that case. It is never a revert.

**The same shape elsewhere.** The creator path's `_seed` (`:1095-1110`) also reads the hook's balance.
There the coin is deployed in the same transaction and held whole by the factory until `_seed` sends
it, so nothing else can be on the hook. `FeastEmissions.burnRemainder` burns strays on purpose and
does not revert on them.

**Tests.** In `test/FeastWindowAuction.t.sol`, which runs again as `FeastWindowAuctionInvertedTest`
with the coin as currency1:

- With 10M FEAST on the hook (above 1% of either delivery), a clearing settle and a late
  no-participation opening each burn exactly what the same settle burns with nothing there, and the
  hook ends holding exactly the stray.
- The opening test prices the coin at a millionth of a quote. Near 1:1, one unit of liquidity holds
  less than a wei and that seed places every wei, so there would be no remainder to burn.
- Against the unfixed factory, both tests revert `PoolTooShallow()`, and so does the PoC.

### `CA-0d9ade0259` — the pricer reads a route pool over the whole window or not at all

**What was wrong.** v1.6.25's Q-3 requires a live ring of 601 before `_vouch` reads a route pool,
because a FULL ring of 601 spans the window. A ring that has just GROWN to 601 is not full: its new
slots are empty and its oldest observation is slot 0. `_vouch` refused only below
`MIN_OBSERVATION_AGE` (300 s) and averaged over `min(age, TWAP_WINDOW)` above it. It answered `Ok`
over 301 seconds in the PoC, which is AstraSec L-2's shape at the pricer.

**What changed** (`FeastPricer.sol:264-283`). History shorter than `TWAP_WINDOW` is `Unpriceable`, the
answer a ring too young always got, and the average is always taken over exactly `TWAP_WINDOW`. A
full ring of 601 spans the window by construction, so a widened ring now waits until its history
does. Nothing else changes. The platform coin's own guard has been the whole window since v1.6.24.

**Tests.** `FeastPricerTest`:

- On the mock: 300 s and 599 s of history are `Unpriceable`, and 600 s is `Ok`.
- On a real ring grown to 601: 301 s and 599 s of history are `Unpriceable`, and 600 s is `Ok`.
- Against the old pricer, both fail, and so does the PoC.

### `CA-6b91a230c6` — a week's grace counts frozen seconds from its close

**What was wrong.** From v1.6.22 a week's pricing grace ended at `close + 72 h + (frozenNow −
anchor)`. The anchor was the previous week's `frozenAtSettle` when that came before the close, else
zero. Frozen seconds that ENDED before the close, which blocked no settlement, still lengthened the
grace — by up to the whole 168-hour budget. For the week before the seam that carries its first
settleable instant past `openAt`, where round 4 H1's rule settles it under the value cap instead of
the pre-launch token cap. Two lapsed 72-hour freezes are enough.

**Why it needed the guardian.** The rule needs the frozen seconds AT the close, and a close is not a
transaction, so nothing can record them. Until v1.6.27 the guardian kept only its latest fold. Once a
freeze that ran after the close had been folded, a history whose frozen seconds all came before the
close read the same as one where some came after. So a rule on that state either counted seconds that
blocked nothing, or let a later freeze reopen a grace that was over. The most favourable such rule was
built as a counterfactual:

- `testFuzz_theGraceNeverEndsEarlyNorReopens_afterASettledWeek` reopened a grace at seed 6195, after
  33 runs.
- One hour of brake taken after the seam week's grace ended carried it past `openAt` again.

The owner ruled for option A on 4 October.

**The guardian's history** (`FeastGuardian.sol:148-160`, `:356`, `:468-487`).

- Every freeze that ends is recorded once, by `_fold`, the one place every freeze ends, as
  `{from, until, frozenBefore}`: three `uint64` in one slot. Nothing else writes the record, and there
  is no setter.
- `frozenSecondsAt(t)` answers `frozenSeconds()` as it read at instant `t`, exactly:
  - a `t` inside or after the live or lapsed freeze reads the live state, with no history;
  - an earlier `t` is a binary search by start over the ended freezes, then one record;
  - an instant not yet reached reads as now.
  - It reads no clock of its own: an instant past the last freeze reads the folded total, and one
    inside the live freeze is capped by `frozenSeconds()`. So `check-boundaries freeze-clocks` counts
    nothing new (`d983d14`; the first spelling's early return on `block.timestamp` had made it 26
    against an allowed 25).
- This was chosen over checkpoints of the running total. Those need two entries per freeze and a
  parity rule to interpolate inside one; here a record answers on its own.
- Growth is bounded by the guardian's own count: every pull costs at least `MIN_FREEZE_CHARGE`, so
  there are at most 168 records and eight probes.
- The record is declared after every other variable, so no slot moves. The sink keeps its O(1)
  subtraction.

**The grace** (`FeastEmissions.sol:302-338`). The anchor is now `frozenSecondsAt(closesAt)`, read
through a one-function interface declared in `FeastEmissions`.

- A freeze that ended before the close extends nothing.
- One running across the close extends the grace by its part after the close only.
- "Never early" and "never again" both hold with the count exact.
- `frozenAtSettle` stays, for the claim grace.

**Gas.** `settle(1)` on the same fixture, before → after:

| freeze history | before | after | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| none | 496,837 | 498,032 | +1,195 |
| four freezes before the close | 496,837 | 503,814 | +6,977 |
| two before the close, two after | 496,837 | 503,776 | +6,939 |
| 32 before the close | 496,837 | 511,275 | +14,438 |

The guardian's endings now write a record:

- `unfreeze`: 35,142 → 80,443 for the first record, and 63,343 after (+28,201);
- `settleLapsed`: 33,572 → 61,617.

There are at most 168 of them in the guardian's life.

**Tests.**

- `FeastPricingGrace.t.sol` keeps properties 1–4 as written and adds a fifth, *exactly*: once three
  open days have passed since the close, the week settles. Both fuzzes check it.
- The two tests that pinned the old cost are restated. A day of brake before the close now holds
  nothing. R4's bound is reached with the whole budget spent after the close.
- New: 144 hours of freezes before week one's close extend nothing; a freeze across the close extends
  the grace by its 62 hours after it; and seed 6195 is a deterministic regression.
- `FeastGuardian.t.sol` reads every boundary of an early lift, a lapse, the live freeze and an unsettled
  lapse. A fuzz checks it against the test's own record of every hold.
- On v1.6.26's emissions, every new grace test is red, and so are both fuzzes (on property 5).

**The PoC's timing was adjusted** (`9e1a6fb`). As the audit lane committed it, the PoC settled at the
OLD grace end, `close + 72 h + 144 h`, which is past `openAt`. There every week is under the value cap,
so no fix to the grace could make that settle a token-cap one: on the fixed tree it fails exactly as
on `57eadbc`.

- It now settles at the corrected grace end, `close + 72 h`, before `openAt`. It asserts the finding's
  substance: the freezes that ended before the close no longer carry the week past the seam, so it
  settles under the pre-launch token cap, and not a second early.
- It is red on v1.6.26 (`QuoteUnpriceable`: the grace still runs 144 hours late).
- The audit lane's record of the original red run stands as the finding's evidence. The control is
  unchanged.

### `CA-a364c3530f` — P22 tolerates the quote's rounding dust, with its range

A clearing settle sends the window's net pot to the hook, and the position takes all but rounding
dust of it, which stays in the hook. The owner ruled it LOW in substance: amend P22 and the test, and
change no code. **The bound was measured before it was written**, because the ruling's "at most 1e6
wei" is not true at every size. In both currency orderings the dust grows as the square root of the
pot:

| pot (BNB) | 1 | 3 | 10 | 1,000 | 1,000,000 | 10,000,000 | 100,000,000 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| quote dust (wei) | 937 | 1,624 | 2,965 | 29,650 | 934,550 | 2,867,244 | 6,282,218 |

Fuzzed before committing, 3,000 runs in each ordering: at most 1e6 wei for every pot from 1 to
1,000,000 BNB, and at most a 1e-15 share of the pot from 1 BNB to `type(uint96).max`. P22's last
sentence (`audit/invariants.yaml`) now reads:

> Nothing the settlement does not place is left owned by nothing, but for the rounding dust of quote
> a clearing settle leaves in the hook: at most 1e6 wei for any pot up to 1,000,000 BNB, and never
> more than a 1e-15 share of the pot at any size. Measured, it grows as the square root of the pot
> (937 wei at 1 BNB, 934,550 at 1,000,000); the window's own test already allowed it
> (`test_theWholePotIsInThePoolAsBidLiquidity`, under 1e6 wei), and it is tolerated rather than
> swept because it is worth less than any transaction that could move it. The coin's remainder is
> burned. Amended in v1.6.27 by the owner's ruling of 4 October 2026 on `CA-a364c3530f`; no code
> changed.

The PoC now asserts at most 1e6 wei and keeps logging the dust, 937. Its name is the proof ledger's
id and is kept. The window suite fuzzes both bounds and reaches the absolute one at the top: 934,550
wei at a million BNB.

### `CA-40dcf992a5` — the tip split, restated as a bounded margin

The v1.6.23 rule tips a press at `max(TIP_BPS, min(floor / largest, TIP_CEILING_BPS))`, where
`largest` is read from the bucket before the press. Each piece of a split press lowers the bucket, so
later pieces are tipped at a higher rate. The owner ruled that the property be restated and the hook
left alone (35 bytes of margin, under review). The PoC now asserts the bounded form on the team's own
fixture:

- Ten presses of a tenth are tipped at most 6% more than one press, the margin
  `test_splittingAPressEarnsNothing` already allows.
- The extra tip, valued at the one press's own rate, is worth less than the gas of the nine extra
  presses at 0.1 gwei, the low end of `docs/button-economics.md`.

Measured: one press is tipped 499,949,504,529,650 and ten pieces 511,567,736,157,263, **+2.32%**.
A tenth-press costs 266,990 gas, so the extra tip, 1.16e13 wei, is a twentieth of the nine presses'
2.40e14 wei of gas.

Stated, not asserted:

- **Two pieces drift +1.28%** (6.41e12 wei). That equals one extra press's gas at about 0.024 gwei,
  and ten pieces break even at about 0.005 gwei.
- Below those gas prices a split nets a fraction of a cent. "Smaller than the gas" is a statement
  about gas prices from about 0.03 gwei up.

The two sentences that said "splitting a press earns nothing", at `src/FeastConstants.sol:197` and
`docs/interface.md:576`, now state this drift (`914442f`, below).
