**M1 — The combined settlement budget still has no distinguishing regression witness.**

The new census assigns `_claimTo`’s shared-budget predicate to two tests. One rejects an unauthorized caller before settlement; the other converts an ordinary creator-only entitlement following an unreferred trade. Neither exercises referral settlement, competing components, the ceiling, or an unpaid combined remainder. [Census assignment](docs/site-census.md:241), [authorization witness](test/TorchAccounting.t.sol:469), [conversion witness](test/TorchAccounting.t.sol:480)

The large-entitlement fixture tests direct creator and referrer claims, but installs no converter and never reaches `_claimTo`. [Fixture](test/TorchClaimBound.t.sol:68)

Two plausible regressions therefore escape these witnesses:

- Delete `room -= owed`: both components receive independent budgets.
- Clear the referral entry instead of subtracting `commission`: the round-11 remainder loss returns at the sibling site. [Budget and debit](src/TorchHook.sol:700)

For example, with `M = MAX_SETTLEMENT`, creator entitlement `M − 1` and referral entitlement `2`, the correct payment is `M`, leaving referral entitlement `1`. The first regression offers `M + 1`; the second destroys the remaining unit. Every existing `claimAsBnb` witness has zero referral entitlement for its claimant.

**The deployed arithmetic is correct on inspection. This finding concerns the missing class-level evidence.** Add connected conversion tests covering referral-only and combined claims below, at and above the ceiling; assert both component remainders, delivered value, repeated draining and rollback after conversion refusal. The two regressions above should be rejected by those tests.
