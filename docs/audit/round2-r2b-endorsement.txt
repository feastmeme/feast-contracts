**HIGH**

**H1 — The oracle still attributes prices to intervals in which they did not prevail.**

A second write at the same timestamp discards the final tick. Buy to tick 2000, then sell back to tick 0 within that timestamp: the oracle retains 2000. After 600 quiet seconds at actual tick 0, its average remains 2000. Moving spot back to 2000 then satisfies the button’s comparison against that false average. [Discarded write](src/TorchOracle.sol:71), [extrapolation](src/TorchOracle.sol:129), [button comparison](src/TorchHook.sol:970)

Both hook-originated purchase paths also change price without recording the resulting tick; callback suppression makes explicit recording necessary. Update the retained tick on same-timestamp writes and record both self-swap results. The existing same-block test checks only observation **count**, so it misses this violation. [Purchase paths](src/TorchHook.sol:517), [adjacent assertion](test/TorchOracle.t.sol:119)

**H2 — Partial conversions and platform purchases still strand funds.**

For a claim of 100 quote units, a first hop spending 10 returns the remaining 90 to the **hook**, whose claim ledger has already retired all 100. The hook neither refunds those 90 to the claimant nor restores entitlement. Subsequent claims measure only their new balance increase, leaving the previous refund unreachable. [Claim retirement and conversion](src/TorchHook.sol:728), [refund recipient](src/TorchConverter.sol:149)

A later partial hop leaves its intermediate token in the converter because only the original input token is refunded. A partial platform purchase similarly leaves WBNB in the hook after fully debiting the source bucket; that action returns only purchased output. [Hop accounting](src/TorchConverter.sol:138), [platform settlement](src/TorchHook.sol:625), [source debit](src/TorchHook.sol:908)

Reconcile every asset at every hop with its beneficiary, or atomically refuse incomplete fills. The deterministic V3 mock always consumes the entire requested input, so its successful conversions cannot exercise these cases. [Mock](test/utils/MockV3Pool.sol:75)

**H3 — Funding a closed epoch after a claim permanently strands the later rewards.**

`creditPot` permits additional funding indefinitely, while `claim` permanently marks a payer finished. Suppose Alice is an epoch’s only payer: credit 100, close the epoch, claim 100, then credit another 100. Alice’s claimable amount remains zero and the additional tokens have no withdrawal path. Anyone can trigger Alice’s first claim, making this a permissionless way to foreclose her later entitlement. [Additional funding](src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:136), [permanent claim flag](src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:146), [permissionless claim](src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:157)

Repeated funding is normal: each platform press processes at most a fraction of pending funds. Track cumulative amounts paid and allow incremental claims, or establish enforceable funding finality before claiming. The integrated test funds once and claims once. [Test](test/TorchPlatformButton.t.sol:204)

**H4 — The required platform pool’s own platform-fee bucket cannot be spent.**

When input is WBNB, every permitted route is impossible: an empty route reverts; a nonempty route must end in WBNB; and revisiting the input token is forbidden. Both hook conversion callers nevertheless invoke the converter unconditionally. [Contradictory route requirements](src/TorchConverter.sol:114), [platform invocation](src/TorchHook.sol:914)

Consequently, the platform coin/WBNB pool’s ordinary 20% platform share accumulates without a usable endpoint. WBNB-denominated creator conversions also fail, although direct claims remain available. Add an already-wrapped-native path. The platform-button fixture creates this pool but tests spending another pool’s quote bucket. [Fixture and tested source](test/TorchPlatformButton.t.sol:98)

**H5 — LP-fee collection can create a permanently unpayable platform bucket.**

Collection calls `_bank`, which attributes the platform share to the **collection epoch**, but never records corresponding payers. Trade in E, collect quote LP fees in otherwise empty E+1, and let E+1 close: its platform bucket is positive while its denominator remains zero. Any conversion producing a positive reward pot reverts at `PotWouldHaveNoClaimants`; that closed denominator cannot subsequently be repaired. [Collection](src/TorchHook.sol:953), [epoch attribution](src/TorchHook.sol:1036), [refusal](src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:139)

If E+1 instead contains a new payer, historical LP income rewards that payer. Define and implement LP-income attribution through its final payment endpoint. The new regression checks collection into buckets, without executing this later conversion. [Test endpoint](test/TorchAccounting.t.sol:245)

**H6 — Dust presses still suppress productive buybacks indefinitely.**

The new self-buyback guard distinguishes zero output from positive output, but places no meaningful minimum on a successful press. An attacker can repeatedly spend a microscopic amount that produces a few coin units, consuming the full hourly cooldown ahead of productive calls. This retains the original denial-of-service mechanism with slightly different dust. [Input checks](src/TorchHook.sol:792), [output and cooldown](src/TorchHook.sol:819)

The platform path additionally accepts a purchase returning zero platform coins after recording its cooldown. [Cooldown](src/TorchHook.sol:899), [unchecked purchased output](src/TorchHook.sol:920)

Require meaningful progress before consuming cooldown, and test dust presses followed by a productive attempt on both buttons.

**M2 — The advertised 3% TWAP bound is still not the implemented bound.**

Three hundred upward ticks represent approximately **3.0453%**. Multiplying square-root price by 0.97 or 1.03 permits terminal price ratios of **0.9409** and **1.0609**, additionally to the accepted initial spot/TWAP displacement. There is no TWAP-derived output minimum. [Claim](src/TorchConstants.sol:96), [implementation](src/TorchConverter.sol:197)

The price-limit test repeats the multiplier, so it confirms implementation consistency while missing the economic discrepancy. Define the intended price/output protection and test it independently. [Test](test/TorchConverterBounds.t.sol:124)

**M3 — Governance documentation misstates both historical-epoch timing and quorum.**

A delayed conversion for epoch E uses the burn share effective **when converted**, despite crediting E’s pot. A change activated in E+2 therefore changes the reward allocation of E’s already-earned fees, contrary to the promise that earned epochs are unaffected. Preserve historical epoch rates or explicitly revise that promise. [Promise](src/TorchHook.sol:848), [current-rate use](src/TorchHook.sol:926)

The application interface also still says quorum is 4% of initial supply. The implementation—and revised §0 ruling—uses `max(0.25% initial supply, 4% snapshot wrapped supply)`. [Interface](docs/interface.md:213), [implementation](src/governance/TorchGovernor.sol:123)

**M4 — The deployment recipe is not executable as written.**

It supplies two constructor arguments for `TorchConverter`, which requires three, and three for `TorchGovernor`, which requires four. It explains predictions for the hook and factory but leaves the future platform-token and timelock addresses required by rewards unresolved. [Recipe](README.md:102), [converter constructor](src/TorchConverter.sol:93), [governor constructor](src/governance/TorchGovernor.sol:58)

Rerunning discovery as instructed also queries the historical pin, so it cannot confirm subsequent cardinality growth. Supply a complete deployment procedure with all immutable links asserted and use an explicit current block for operational rechecks. [Recheck instruction](README.md:91), [default block](tools/discover_quotes.py:245)

**M5 — The quote survey still overstates oracle failure, readiness, and identity.**

An old cardinality-one observation can support a legitimate constant-price TWAP. Spot equalling TWAP does not prove fallback; the pinned test itself successfully reads a 600-second average and records that the observation is substantially older. Cardinality refusal is Torch’s additional policy, rather than proof that such history is false. [Pinned evidence](test/TorchConverter.t.sol:99)

The survey’s `priceable` predicate checks cardinality alone, omitting history age and spot/TWAP deviation. Its output therefore cannot establish that conversion will be accepted. Matching a self-reported symbol and finding liquidity also cannot authenticate an issuer or exclude liquid counterfeits. [Predicate](tools/discover_quotes.py:201), [readiness claim](tools/discover_quotes.py:305), [identity claim](README.md:75)

The fork fixtures’ **provisional** labels are honest. Narrow the survey’s conclusions and retain independent address confirmation.

[Decimals promise](docs/plan.md:339), [duration promise](docs/plan.md:600), [duration handling](src/TorchVesting.sol:54), [fee constant](src/TorchConstants.sol:70)

**M7 — Published allocation and incentive claims remain mathematically false.**

The platform coin’s fees do not “burn entirely”: its 20% platform bucket remains, rewards receive a governed portion, and tips remain payable. [Claim](README.md:109), [separate platform allocation](src/TorchHook.sol:1009), [reward split](src/TorchHook.sol:924)

The claimed maximum 10% rebate/90% wash-trading loss also contradicts the permitted burn-share band: at 30% burn, the reward allocation is 14% of the original fee before tips and execution costs. The test itself permits 14%, despite its ninety-percent title. Neither future token valuation nor guaranteed keeper profitability follows from that arithmetic. [Claim](src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:14), [test](test/TorchEpochRewards.t.sol:213)

Tips can round to zero, contradicting the promised non-dust floor. [Promise](docs/plan.md:295), [division](src/TorchHook.sol:829)

**M8 — Application liquidity-sizing guidance fails away from the fixture price.**

Liquidity is generally not approximately equal to token allocation. With coin as currency0 and an opening square-root price around 2, a position extending upward consumes approximately `L/2` coins. Following `L ≈ allocation` leaves roughly half unused and triggers `PoolTooShallow`. Publish price- and ordering-dependent formulas and the tick-spacing requirements. [Guidance](docs/interface.md:55), [range and remainder check](src/TorchFactory.sol:135)

**M9 — Predicted launch addresses still do not bind the intended economic configuration.**

Another caller can front-run a creation using identical name, symbol, supply and salt, while replacing the creator, quote, shares or allocation. These changes preserve the predicted coin address, and single-sided creation requires no supplied quote. The intended launch then fails CREATE2 while its predicted address carries the attacker’s configuration. This also matters when prewiring the platform-token address. Bind the deployment salt to the intended configuration or an authenticated creator namespace. [Creation](src/TorchFactory.sol:105), [address derivation](src/TorchFactory.sol:182)

**M10 — README omits the required external-authority disclosure.**

At this pin, README has no “What torch does not control” section and does not disclose either the venue’s pause authority or mutable LP-fee share. Its opening says there is no pause. The disclosures exist in `docs/interface.md`, but the plan expressly requires them in README. Add them there; the underlying venue authorities themselves are accepted boundaries. [README claim](README.md:8), [requirement](docs/plan.md:378), [existing interface disclosure](docs/interface.md:221)
