**HIGH**

**H1 — The progress predicate measures the request, while the clock accepts a smaller realized fill.**

The minimum check compares `amountIn` against 1% of `pending`. The buyback then accepts partial fills, debits `quoteSpent`, and starts the hour whenever `coinOut != 0`. It never requires `quoteSpent >= minimum`. [Request check](src/TorchHook.sol:763), [realized settlement](src/TorchHook.sol:532), [debit and clock](src/TorchHook.sol:775).

A sufficiently large accumulated bucket relative to liquidity executable within the price limit therefore admits a request meeting the floor whose actual consumption falls below it. Positive output is sufficient to occupy the hour. No invariant ties accumulated fees to current executable depth.

The new tests exercise undersized **requests**. The purported partial-fill test merely asserts spending is *at most* the request; it never establishes a partial fill. [Progress test](test/TorchBuyback.t.sol:339), [partial-fill test](test/TorchBuyback.t.sol:212).

Require the realized debit to satisfy the progress predicate before accepting the press. Exercise a genuine partial fill and assert that insufficient progress reverts the settlement, bucket, oracle update and clock together. If “at least 1%” is literal, round the minimum upward.

**MEDIUM**

**M1 — Direct claims retire entitlement without verifying delivery.**

A creator/referrer claim retires its nominal entitlement, burns the corresponding vault claim and requests a token transfer. It never checks the recipient’s balance increase. An outgoing transfer-tax token can deliver 90 units while permanently settling entitlement of 100. [Claim accounting](src/TorchHook.sol:606), [payout settlement](src/TorchHook.sol:585).

This contradicts the explicit promise of balance-delta protection against a quote token becoming fee-on-transfer after deployment. [Promise](docs/plan.md:226).

Enforce exact delivery or explicitly withdraw that guarantee. Existing pause tests exercise reversion, not successful short delivery.

**M2 — A competing launch can occupy the predicted address with different economics.**

CREATE2 commits to name, symbol, supply and salt, but excludes creator, quote, fee shares, allocation, vesting duration and opening configuration. Another caller can copy the committed fields and deploy first with different economics. [Creation](src/TorchFactory.sol:120), [prediction](src/TorchFactory.sol:182).

The regression checks identical-parameter reuse, not competing launch intent. [Test](test/TorchFactory.t.sol:270). Bind deployment identity to authenticated intent and the economic configuration.

**M3 — The advertised 3% TWAP bound exceeds what execution actually guarantees.**

The hook multiplies **square-root price** by 0.97 or 1.03. Those permit terminal price ratios of 0.9409 or 1.0609 relative to spot, before the allowed spot/TWAP displacement. [Limit](src/TorchHook.sol:505).

For conversion, three adverse 300-tick displacements followed by the permitted 3% shortfall against spot-derived expected output allow approximately:

`0.97 / 1.0001^900 = 0.8865`

of the corresponding TWAP-priced output, before pool fees. [Expectation](src/TorchConverter.sol:150), [output floor](src/TorchConverter.sol:184).

These protections do not establish the stated maximum move from the time-weighted price. [Claim](src/TorchConstants.sol:121). Define and test the actual economic bound, or narrow the documentation.

**M4 — The deployment recipe remains non-executable as written.**

The recipe specifies `TorchConverter(v3Factory, wrappedNative)`, but its constructor requires the predicted hook address as a third argument. [Recipe](README.md:130), [constructor](src/TorchConverter.sol:112).

The fork fixture correctly predicts hook at deployment nonce `n + 3` and factory at `n + 5`, and supplies the missing argument. That working procedure should be the recipe. [Fixture](test/TorchEndToEnd.t.sol:102).

The instruction to rerun discovery after growing an observation ring also defaults to rereading the historical pin. It cannot observe the new transactions without an explicit later block selection. [Recheck instruction](README.md:124), [default block](tools/discover_quotes.py:245).

**M6 — Quote discovery establishes neither issuer identity nor converter readiness.**

Discovery labels a pool `priceable` from cardinality alone. Conversion also requires sufficient observation age and an acceptable spot/TWAP deviation. Nevertheless, the tool calls these pools ones the converter would accept. [Discovery predicate](tools/discover_quotes.py:219), [reported conclusion](tools/discover_quotes.py:305), [execution checks](src/TorchConverter.sol:221).

The cardinality-one evidence establishes average/spot equality at one snapshot and explicitly records that the observation is old. Likewise, a matching self-reported symbol and available liquidity cannot authenticate an issuer or exclude liquid counterfeits. [Verification](tools/discover_quotes.py:189).

The end-to-end fixture’s **PROVISIONAL** label is honest. Preserve independent address confirmation, describe discovery as candidate discovery, and align readiness labels with execution. [Label](test/TorchEndToEnd.t.sol:37).

**L2 — Public oracle consultation accepts windows below the advertised floor.**

`consult` checks stored-history age, but accepts a one-second requested window on a mature oracle. Zero reaches division by zero. The shipped buyback uses the fixed 600-second wrapper, limiting immediate impact. [Consultation](src/TorchOracle.sol:106).

Validate the effective window or narrow the public API’s promise.

**L3 — Buyback events report requested input as actual spending.**

The event field is named `quoteSpent`, but emission supplies `amountIn`. A successful partial fill therefore overstates expenditure to indexers while correctly debiting the smaller realized amount. [Declaration](src/TorchHook.sol:94), [emission](src/TorchHook.sol:792).

Emit `quoteSpent` and verify it under a genuine partial fill.

**Completeness of the shipped mandate**

The full original product is deliberately reduced. The accepted platform/rewards/governance cut is documented and is not a finding.

| Fee share | Destination in v1 |
|---|---|
| Self-burn | Vault claims fund the coin purchase; the keeper receives a tip and the remainder is burned. Realized spending preserves unspent entitlement, but H1 defeats the progress promise. |
| Creator | Quote-token pull claim, or claimant-authorized conversion to **wrapped** native. Burn-address creators redirect to self-burn. Successful short delivery remains M1. |
| Referrer | Quote-token pull claim or claimant-authorized conversion. Unreferred commissions remain with the creator. |
| Platform | Accumulates in vault-backed accounting with no v1 spending path, exactly as the accepted cut declares. |

These destinations are implemented by the [banking logic](src/TorchHook.sol:877), [claim paths](src/TorchHook.sol:606), and [buyback settlement](src/TorchHook.sol:768).

LP quote income is collected; its platform portion redirects to self-burn. LP coin income goes to the burn address. Vesting pays the fixed recipient over its configured schedule. [LP disposition](src/TorchHook.sol:565), [LP platform redirection](src/TorchHook.sol:904).

The converter’s per-hop refusal is atomic and balance-independent. I found no new remainder stranded by that boundary: failed conversion preserves the quote claim, and direct claiming remains available. There is no surviving two-platform-press state transition to assess. [Per-hop enforcement](src/TorchConverter.sol:262).

Documented live entry-point signatures match the source and checked-in ABIs. The deployment constructor discrepancy and behavioural documentation issues remain. I would not endorse internal v1 until H1 and the remaining delivery, documentation and evidence defects are resolved.
