**HIGH**

**H1 — Rewards accept additional funding after permanently closing a payer’s entitlement.**

`creditPot` can increase a closed epoch’s pot indefinitely, while `claim` permanently sets a boolean. No funding-finalization condition connects these operations. Evidence: [src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:136](src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:136), [src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:157](src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:157).

Construction: Alice and Bob pay 75% and 25% of an epoch’s fees. An initial conversion funds 100 platform tokens. After closure, anyone claims Alice’s 75. Further capped conversions of that same epoch’s remaining bucket add 900 tokens. Bob then receives 250; Alice cannot receive her additional 675 because her flag is already set. Those 675 tokens remain permanently unclaimable.

An attacker can force the early claim because the payer need not be the caller. The platform button explicitly supports spending old epoch buckets, so this is an ordinary reachable sequence, not a forged recorder call. [src/TorchHook.sol:886](src/TorchHook.sol:886), [src/TorchHook.sol:933](src/TorchHook.sol:933).

**H2 — The oracle still accepts prices that did not prevail during the reported interval.**

The hook writes after ordinary swaps, but the oracle discards every subsequent write at the same timestamp. It then extrapolates the first retained tick. Evidence: [src/TorchHook.sol:361](src/TorchHook.sol:361), [src/TorchOracle.sol:72](src/TorchOracle.sol:72), [src/TorchOracle.sol:128](src/TorchOracle.sol:128).

Construction, after the cap window: start at tick 0 with an eligible bucket. At timestamp T, buy to tick 2000, then sell back to tick 0 in the same transaction. The retained observation says 2000. Leave the actual pool at 0 for 600 seconds. At T+600, buy to 2000 and press the button. Its spot and reported TWAP both equal 2000, although the actual preceding window was at 0. Without that final manipulation, an honest press at the stable price is refused.

Button swaps also change the pool without recording their resulting tick: their callbacks are correctly suppressed by the venue, and neither button writes explicitly afterward. This is another missing observation, not a claim that the venue re-enters the hook. [src/TorchHook.sol:810](src/TorchHook.sol:810), [src/TorchHook.sol:920](src/TorchHook.sol:920).

**H3 — Successful partial conversions still retire entitlement and strand the remainder.**

Three reachable forms remain:

- A creator claims 100 quote through `claimAsBnb`; the first hop consumes 10 and produces positive output. The converter refunds 90 to its caller—the hook—but the hook has already retired all 100 and neither pays nor recredits the 90.
- In quote → intermediate → native, a partially filled second hop leaves intermediate tokens in the converter. Its refund code examines only the original input token.
- Platform buyback retires the requested source amount before conversion. Refunded quote remains unaccounted in the hook; a partially filled final platform purchase additionally leaves native tokens there without restoring entitlement.

Evidence: [src/TorchHook.sol:728](src/TorchHook.sol:728), [src/TorchHook.sol:747](src/TorchHook.sol:747), [src/TorchConverter.sol:140](src/TorchConverter.sol:140), [src/TorchConverter.sol:153](src/TorchConverter.sol:153), [src/TorchHook.sol:910](src/TorchHook.sol:910), [src/TorchHook.sol:634](src/TorchHook.sol:634).

Mature pools with liquidity that reaches the supplied price limit before consuming the request satisfy the construction. Positive output makes these calls succeed. The self-buyback reconciliation does not cover these paths.

**H4 — A two-hop attacker-owned route can extract a platform bucket while passing every price check.**

The platform button accepts anyone’s route. The converter permits arbitrary intermediate tokens and checks each supplied pool against its own history; it imposes no independent bound on the route’s input-to-native value. Evidence: [src/TorchHook.sol:852](src/TorchHook.sol:852), [src/TorchConverter.sol:119](src/TorchConverter.sol:119), [src/TorchConverter.sol:177](src/TorchConverter.sol:177).

Construction: an attacker creates token X and genuine venue pools Q/X and X/WBNB. They establish sufficient retained history at constant prices, with cardinality at least two. Supply X-side liquidity to Q/X and a small amount of native liquidity to X/WBNB. Choose prices so 100 valuable Q buys approximately 100 X, but 100 X buys only approximately 0.0001 WBNB. Both spot/TWAP deviations can be zero, and sufficient liquidity keeps both fills inside their limits.

Press an eligible source bucket through Q → X → WBNB. The attacker’s first LP position receives the valuable Q, which they can withdraw; the protocol receives negligible native and subsequently negligible genuine platform tokens. The route has two hops and no repeated token. The hourly cap limits the amount per press, not the extraction rate from that amount.

**H5 — LP collection recreates historical-fee theft or permanently unspendable epoch buckets.**

Collection banks accumulated LP fees using the collection epoch. It does not record their original payers—or any payer. `_bank` nevertheless creates a platform allocation for that epoch. Evidence: [src/TorchHook.sol:953](src/TorchHook.sol:953), [src/TorchHook.sol:1036](src/TorchHook.sol:1036). Payer recording exists separately in `_accrue`: [src/TorchHook.sol:988](src/TorchHook.sol:988).

Construction: meaningful LP fees accumulate in E. In E+1, a stranger collects them:

- If E+1 closes without fee-paying swaps for that quote, its platform bucket has no denominator. Every later conversion producing a nonzero reward pot reverts at `PotWouldHaveNoClaimants`; the platform allocation is permanently stuck.
- If the attacker makes the only small fee-paying trade in E+1, they receive the reward allocation funded by historical LP fees.

The zero-denominator guard moves the orphaning from the rewards contract back into the hook; it does not establish that every bucket has eligible payers. [src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:139](src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:139).

**H6 — Wrapped-native platform buckets have no accepted spending path.**

Both conversion callers invoke the converter even when their input already equals wrapped native. Empty routes revert. Every nonempty valid route must finish in wrapped native, but the no-revisit predicate rejects any output token equal to the input. Therefore the accepted route set for wrapped-native input is empty. Evidence: [src/TorchConverter.sol:115](src/TorchConverter.sol:115), [src/TorchConverter.sol:120](src/TorchConverter.sol:120), [src/TorchConverter.sol:125](src/TorchConverter.sol:125).

Construction: trade the required platform-coin/WBNB pool, then attempt to spend its platform allocation. Every route fails at [src/TorchHook.sol:916](src/TorchHook.sol:916). The same over-refusal affects WBNB-denominated `claimAsBnb`, although plain claimant payouts remain available.

Round 1’s unnecessary round-trip requirement has become a permanent refusal after adding the cycle prohibition.

**H7 — Zero-output platform purchases still consume funds and the cooldown.**

The platform button checks that conversion produced native, but never checks that the final purchase produced platform tokens. It records the cooldown and retires source entitlement beforehand. Evidence: [src/TorchHook.sol:903](src/TorchHook.sol:903), [src/TorchHook.sol:917](src/TorchHook.sol:917), [src/TorchHook.sol:921](src/TorchHook.sol:921).

Construction: on mature eligible pools, choose a small source amount whose conversion returns one raw native unit. The final exact-input venue swap can consume that unit entirely as its rounded LP fee and return zero platform coin. `bought`, tip, burn and pot allocation are all zero; the call nevertheless succeeds and blocks productive presses for an hour. Repeat at expiry.

The self button’s new zero-output guard also still accepts a dust-positive purchase with zero tip, allowing inexpensive cooldown occupation. The platform zero-output case reproduces the original defect directly.

**MEDIUM**

**M1 — Governance changes the split applied to already-earned epochs.**

The setter defers its global value until the next epoch, but old fee buckets use the value at conversion time. Construction: fees accrue in E with a 50% burn share; a matured proposal schedules 70% for E+1. Converting E’s remaining bucket during E+1 burns 70% and rewards 30%, changing the treatment of fees already earned under the previous split.

No historical split is retained or selected using the supplied earning epoch. Evidence: [src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:86](src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:86), [src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:111](src/TorchEpochRewards.sol:111), [src/TorchHook.sol:926](src/TorchHook.sol:926). The band and authority restrictions hold; earning-epoch isolation does not.

**M2 — Factory addresses remain vulnerable to configuration substitution by front-running.**

The CREATE2 address binds name, symbol, supply and salt, but not caller, creator, quote, shares or allocation. An attacker copies a pending launch’s address-determining inputs, substitutes their creator and economic configuration, and supplies valid single-sided liquidity parameters. Creation requires no quote contribution. The intended launch then fails because the address is occupied.

Evidence: [src/TorchFactory.sol:105](src/TorchFactory.sol:105), [src/TorchFactory.sol:120](src/TorchFactory.sol:120), [src/TorchFactory.sol:182](src/TorchFactory.sol:182). This remains especially consequential for a platform token whose predicted address is already embedded in rewards.

**M3 — Cardinality refusal still excludes valid constant-price history.**

A v3 pool with one retained observation older than the requested window can provide a valid constant-price TWAP. The converter refuses it regardless. Equality between spot and TWAP does not establish missing history.

Evidence: [src/TorchConverter.sol:188](src/TorchConverter.sol:188). The pinned fork test itself obtains the 600-second observation successfully and establishes that the sole observation exceeds the age floor before asserting refusal: [test/TorchConverter.t.sol:105](test/TorchConverter.t.sol:105), [test/TorchConverter.t.sol:111](test/TorchConverter.t.sol:111). R9’s “any pool with enough history” acceptance requirement remains unmet.

**M4 — The implemented limits do not enforce the advertised 3% TWAP slippage band.**

Three hundred ticks represent approximately 3.0453%. Multiplying square-root price by 0.97 or 1.03 permits terminal price ratios of 0.9409 or 1.0609, measured from an initial spot that may already deviate from TWAP. A mature pool at an allowed adverse spot, followed by a purchase moving farther toward its limit, therefore passes while execution falls outside the advertised TWAP band.

Evidence: [src/TorchConstants.sol:96](src/TorchConstants.sol:96), [src/TorchConverter.sol:204](src/TorchConverter.sol:204), [src/TorchHook.sol:525](src/TorchHook.sol:525). The regression asserts the same square-root multiplier rather than an economic output bound: [test/TorchConverterBounds.t.sol:126](test/TorchConverterBounds.t.sol:126).

For either token ordering, `buyingCoin = zeroForOne != coinIsCurrency0` selects the quote correctly. Fully filled fee arithmetic is:

| Trade | Fee |
|---|---|
| Exact-input buy | `floor(grossQuote / 101)` |
| Exact-output sell | `floor(netQuote / 99)` |
| Exact-input sell | `floor(actualPoolQuoteOut / 100)` |
| Exact-output buy | `floor(actualPoolQuoteIn / 100)` |

An integer sweep over 99,999 input values confirmed that specified buys can undercharge `floor(poolQuote/100)` by one raw unit; specified sells match it exactly. Repeating dust trades can repeatedly obtain the truncation benefit. Share-rounding remainders accrue to the creator—or self-burn for a burn-address creator. Tip rounding favors remaining output, burn-split rounding favors rewards, and pro-rata rounding leaves pot dust.

**Reentrancy and frozen quotes:** no finding assumes callbacks during hook-originated swaps. The venue exclusion is present. [lib/infinity-core/src/libraries/Hooks.sol:71](lib/infinity-core/src/libraries/Hooks.sol:71) Direct payouts retire entitlement before external transfers; transfer refusal rolls the transaction back. Self-buyback settles quote internally using vault claims, so quote blacklisting does not interrupt that settlement. Platform conversion and direct payouts do transfer quote and can be delayed by its issuer. Failed attempts cost the caller gas; no separate protocol gas reimbursement exists.

**Cap documentation:** intermediate contracts under the same `tx.origin` do not bypass the counter. The “contract-per-swap” limitation stated in R5 therefore remains inaccurate; distinct origins are the relevant limitation.
