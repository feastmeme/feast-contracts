## The fixes

**M-3** (owner: fix; the coordinator's verification rates it Low, measured). Outside the band
`offered <= held < 2 * offered`, `_price` answers the start price, floored, and writes nothing. On
v1.6.24 `take` never wrote the descent's clock either, so the curve `open` had anchored kept running
while the quote read the top, and a lot brought back into the band came back onto a curve nobody
could see. Measured on `2ebf5ef` (PoC `2cd386f`):

- double a lot of 100e18 and wait 80 hours;
- `take(1)` costs exactly 2^64 wei and leaves `anchor` and `anchoredAt` untouched;
- the quote drops from 2^192 to 4.904e35, the 80-hour curve floored by that one-unit sale;
- the remaining 199.99e18 units sweep for 288,230,376,151,711,744 wei — 18.735 FEAST in all.

The same lot left alone sells whole for 1,525,878,906,250,000 wei, so the attack costs the attacker
far more than waiting. The harm is concealment: for 80 hours nobody else could see a price to bid at.

*The fix* (`FeastPlatformSink.sol:481-486`). Before it prices, a take that finds the lot outside the
band restarts its curve with `open`'s three writes:

- `anchor = SINK_START_PRICE_Q128`;
- `anchoredAt =` now;
- `frozenAtAnchor = guardian.frozenSeconds()`.

The charge does not change. It is `_atLeastTheFloor(lot, START)` either way, the quote the view
published, and from then on the stored curve agrees with it. Two of `open`'s other writes are not
made:

- **The floor stays.** A take may raise the floor and never lower it (v1.6.3). The quote it is
  charged includes the floor (round 1 H2), so clearing it would sell under the published price.
- **The size is never raised.** `offered` is lowered to `held` when the lot holds less than its
  descent priced (an issuer shrank it), and left alone otherwise. Only `open` sets the size a descent
  prices (round 1 H1).

*The shape differs from the brief's in one write, and why.* The brief's shape also wrote
`offered = held`, as `open` does. Both close scenario 1 identically: the same 2^64 wei entry, a lot
left priced at the top, and the planned sweep refused at its own price. They differ only where the
size would have grown, and there the brief's shape brings back the round-5 defect. This was measured
by running the brief's shape exactly, as a counterfactual, against the full suite (123 suites, 854
passed, 5 failed). The deciding failure is the pre-existing round-5 witness
`test_aCarriedFloorIsClearedByOpeningAndNotByASale`, which reverts `AlreadyOpened`. The sequence:

1. An emptied lot keeps the floor its last whole-lot sale left: 2^186 after a one-unit lot is bought
   at the top.
2. A release refills it. The refill reads at the top, and the keeper's `open` is what clears the
   carried floor along with the lot it belonged to.
3. Under the brief's shape, one unit taken at the top (2^64 wei) re-anchors over the refill and puts
   it back in the band, under the carried floor.
4. The keeper's `open` then reverts, and the refill cannot sell below that floor until the floor's
   own daily halving brings it down: 58 days to parity.

Lowering the size can only bring a SHRUNKEN lot back into the band, never a bigger lot under a
smaller lot's floor. So the shipped shape is the brief's, with its fourth write restricted to the
rule this contract already documents: a take may lower the size, never raise it.
`test_aRestartingTakeLeavesTheSizeAndTheKeepersOpen` pins it.

*Why `_price`, a view, is left as is.* It cannot write the restart, and it does not need to. Outside
the band it already answers exactly what the restarted curve says at the moment of the take. What was
wrong was never the published number; it was the stored curve under it, and only a write can change
that. The first write that sees the lot outside the band is the take that would otherwise have sold
on the stale curve. A view also cannot see the history needed for the one case left, next.

*Why the floor is kept.* Clearing it would hand nobody a new power: whenever the restart fires,
`open` is permitted on the same predicate and does clear the floor. The deciding reasons are the
two above:

- the take is charged the floored quote and must not sell under it
  (`test_aRestartingTakeIsChargedTheQuoteTheViewPublished` pins a floor above the start price,
  charged to the unit);
- "a take never lowers the floor" is the v1.6.3 invariant, whose witness kills "clearing the floor
  on a take".

*What it cannot reach.* Scenario 2 (shrink, wait, one wei back in) has no take between the lot
leaving the band and coming back. The sink sees no write at which it could notice, and no view
could either. It needs an asset that can shrink a holder's balance. It is the limit `docs/plan.md`
B9 has declared since v1.6.2, witnessed by `test_theDeclaredLimitOfWhatAShrunkenLotCanBeToldFrom`;
the mitigation is a keeper that opens a lot the moment it holds less than its descent priced. The
revived curve is, to the wei, the one the lot would have been on had it never shrunk:
1,525,878,906,250,000 wei for the whole lot, the control's price.
`test_lowerSide_anArrivalWithNoTakeIsStillB9sDeclaredLimit` asserts the limit. The same shape
through a take, where a one-unit take brings the shrunken lot back, is closed
(`test_lowerSide_oneUnitAtTheTopRestartsTheCurveOverWhatIsHere`).

Nobody gains a power: `open` is permitted on exactly the restart's condition and writes more. The
restart also puts no chord above a descended price (the v1.6.19 ruling's concern), because it
restarts AT the price the view was already publishing.

**Q-3** (owner: fix). `_vouch` accepted a v3 ring of two observations. A pool writes on an in-range
mint or burn and on a swap that moves the tick, so one in-range write just after a trade left both
observations inside 300 s. The quote read `Unpriceable`, and once `PRICING_GRACE` was over,
`FeastEmissions.settle` (`:402-403`) counted that quote's week as zero. This was measured on
`2ebf5ef` with a pool that restates the venue's observation rules (`test/utils/RingV3Pool.sol`):
one write per second at most, growth only at the end of the ring, the oldest observation being the
slot after the newest, and `observe` interpolating and refusing `OLD`.

- **`FeastConstants.MIN_RING_CARDINALITY = TWAP_WINDOW + 1 = 601`** (`FeastConstants.sol:277`). It
  is chosen from the window and the pool's write rate. A v3 pool writes at most one observation per
  block timestamp (`Oracle.write` returns early when its newest observation carries this second),
  and timestamps are whole seconds. So a full ring of `n` slots spans at least `n - 1` seconds,
  whoever writes and however short the blocks, and at 601 the oldest observation is always a whole
  window old. No write pattern can make the history too short, and the average is never shortened.
  Both sides of the bound are witnessed on the ring model: a ring written every second spans exactly
  600 s at 601 slots and 599 s at 600.
- **The read** (`FeastPricer.sol:261`): `_vouch` reads a live ring below 601 as `Unpriceable`. This
  is deterministic from the day of listing, instead of triggered by whoever writes before
  settlement. The existing `cardinality < 2` check (`:252`) stays above it, as the arithmetic's own
  floor.
- **The listing check** (`FeastPricer.sol:117-120`): the constructor refuses a route pool whose ring
  has not been asked for 601, with `RingTooShallow(quote, pool, cardinalityNext, required)`. It reads
  `observationCardinalityNext`, which is what a lister controls and what the venue never lets shrink.
  It is cheap: one `slot0()` per pool, in constructor code only. The runtime grows by the read alone
  (+24 bytes), and the initcode by +298. The pricer is deployed by `DeployPackage2`, not by the
  factory or the hook, so neither moves a byte.
- **Operating rule.** Call `increaseObservationCardinalityNext(601)` on every routed pool before
  listing it. Anyone may, and the caller pays one fresh storage slot per observation added. The pool
  takes the new depth at the first write that reaches the end of its old ring, and until then the
  quote is `Unpriceable`, visibly. `docs/interface.md` says so in the emissions section.

The written rule that an `Unpriceable` quote counts zero after the grace (`Q11`) is unchanged. What
changed is that a listed, compliant quote cannot be put there by a write:

- with the minimum, the PoC's exact front-run leaves the week priced
  (`test_theFrontRunThatZeroedAWeekDoesNotReachARingOfTheMinimum`);
- so does a write every second for a window and a half
  (`test_noWritePatternMakesARingOfTheMinimumTooYoung`).

For the pricer, this also retires v1.6.24's declared boundary that `_vouch` shortens the window to a
pool's history. Once the ring is full, no write can shorten it; what remains is the first ten
minutes after a ring is widened, which nobody can renew.

**Not changed:** `FeastConverter` keeps its own floor of 2 (`MIN_ORACLE_CARDINALITY`). A ring it
cannot read makes a conversion revert, which the claimant may retry or avoid by claiming in the
quote; nothing is zeroed, and M-1's `minOut` bounds the outcome. The converter is also outside this
lane's write surface.

## Answered

**L-3 — bullet 1 accepted, by design; bullet 2 already closed.** `platformSink` is written once, at
genesis (`FeastFactory.sol:283`, `:609`), and the hook reads it live on every release
(`FeastHook.sol:1639`). A settable fee destination is exactly the admin key the product says it does
not have. An address that can redirect every pool's platform fees is more dangerous than the failure
it would insure against, and it would make "no admin keys, non-upgradeable" untrue. The residual is
owned: if the sink were defective, the guardian's time-boxed freeze buys time (72 h a pull, 168 h in
all, gone 90 days after genesis). After that, fees reach a sink that can spend them on nothing but a
descending auction anyone may buy from.

Bullet 2 ("FEAST inside the window has no exit after `openAt`") no longer describes the code. Since
v1.6.24:

- `settle` has no deadline (`_canSettle`, `FeastLaunchWindow.sol:536-539`);
- the auction expires at `abandonAt()` (`:561`: `endsAt + ABANDON_AFTER + frozenSeconds`);
- after that, `settle` takes the no-participation branch (`:272`): the pool opens, the coin reaches
  it, and every commit is refundable.

No state strands the coin for want of a timely call (`M2_SettleLockup.t.sol`).

**L-4 — answered: already in place.**

- The three emissions levers, `setValueRatioBps`, `setRedirect` and `setPricer`
  (`FeastEmissions.sol:207`, `:218`, `:228`), are `onlyTimelock` (`:201`) against an immutable
  `TimelockController` (`:126`). It has a three-day delay, the team multisig as its only proposer,
  and no admin but itself.
- `assignPartner` requires `msg.sender == guardian.guardian()`, the team multisig, and otherwise
  reverts `NotTheTeamMultisig` (`FeastGenesisGrants.sol:158-160`). It fills each empty slot once,
  within the 90-day window.
- The guardian's powers are bounded at the callee: `FREEZE_MAX` 72 h, `FREEZE_BUDGET` 168 h,
  `GUARDIAN_SUNSET` 90 days (`FeastGuardian.sol:64`, `:70`, `:73`). After the sunset, `expire()`
  (`:322`) lets anyone end the power.

`freeze()` and `stopCreation()` are deliberately not timelocked. A delayed brake is not a brake, so
the bound is a time budget instead.

**Q-1 — intended, since v1.6.20.** A fill at the floor leaves the next price at the floor while the
raised anchor is still below it. This is `P3` MEDIUM of the v1.6.20 round, carried, with its red run
recorded in `test/audit/poc/FloorSalePriceRise.t.sol` (skipped so the gate stays honest); it was
measured identically on v1.6.19. The floor is the platform's reservation price, and a sale struck at
it is no evidence about anything above it. The obvious hardening, restarting the floor's clock on a
fill at the floor, would let one-wei fills every 24 hours pin the floor for ever, the mirror of
`CA-b229a31396`. To be safe it would have to be share-weighted too. No change.

**Q-2 — the premise is gone in v1.6.24.** Its chain begins "if `settle()` fails after `openAt`".
Since v1.6.24, `settle` has no deadline (`_canSettle`, `FeastLaunchWindow.sol:536-539`) and no
arithmetic revert. A pot below `MIN_POT`, an unrepresentable price, and abandonment (`abandonAt()`,
`:561`) all take the no-participation branch. The cascade the report describes is real:
emissions and grant unlocks do wait on the pool. But what they wait on is one permissionless
`settle()`, which anyone may make at any time, not an hour that can be missed. Halting is the
written safe side (`FeastEmissions.sol:552-557`): emissions must not price a week against a venue
that does not exist yet. The hardening is operational: a keeper that calls `settle()` once after
`endsAt`.
