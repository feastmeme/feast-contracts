# v1.6.28 — AstraSec's two suggestions of 7 October: the guard on the three functions that pay, and its slot named

## The two suggestions

### 1 — the guard's transient slot has a name

**What changed** (`FeastHook.sol:133-136`). `uint256 private constant REENTRANCY_GUARD_SLOT = 0`.
The modifier's clearing `tstore` (`:177`) and `_enter()`'s `tload` and `tstore` (`:183`, `:187`) read
it instead of the literal `0`.

**Why it moves no byte.** Inline assembly reads a value-type constant as its literal. Each site is
still the one-byte `PUSH0` that the v1.6.24 guard was priced on. This was measured, not assumed. The
hook built from `2002ad5` is byte-identical to the one built from `a92b530`: 24,558 bytes, sha256
`ece55edc3d19fb15…`, keccak `0xf30b8746…`. As a negative control, the same build with the constant
set to 1 gives 24,563 bytes and another hash, so the comparison can see a change at these sites. The
slot stays 0, which nothing else in the hook uses. Its three other transient slots, `FILL_COIN_SLOT`,
`FILL_QUOTE_SLOT` and `PREBUY_POOL_SLOT`, are keccak-derived.

### 2 — the guard is on the three functions that pay

**What changed.** The coordinator's four-line patch, applied with `git apply --3way`.
`claimCreator` (`:1482`), `claimReferrer` (`:1495`) and `releasePlatformFees` (`:1675`) gain
`nonReentrant`, and `claim` (`:1546`) loses it. Seven entry points now share the one flag: the three
that pay, plus `claimAsBnb`, `buyBackAndBurn`, `burnFilledCoin` and `collectLpFees` as in v1.6.24.
`releasePlatformFees` was not guarded before. The guard's comment (`:138-172`) and `claim`'s
(`:1542-1545`) now say so.

**Why the shape is safe.**

- **`claim` is unguarded and calls two guarded functions in sequence** (`:1549-1550`). `claimCreator`
  takes the flag, and its modifier clears it after the body (`:177`). So `claimReferrer` finds the
  flag down and takes it in turn.
  - If `claim` were still guarded, the first inner call would refuse itself. If the flag were left
    standing, the second would.
  - `claim` pays nothing itself and moves nothing between the two calls.
  - `test_claimPaysBothSharesThroughTheTwoGuardedFunctionsInTurn` pays an account owed both shares
    with one `claim`. A re-entrant `claim` from inside either payout is refused by name, one call
    down.
- **`claimAsBnb` never nests with them.** It keeps its own guard and pays through `_claimTo`
  (`:1607`, `:1610`). `_claimTo` debits both shares and calls `_release` directly, never
  `claimCreator` or `claimReferrer`. `test_claimAsBnbPaysACreatorWhoIsAlsoAReferrerInOneCall` pays a
  creator who is also a referrer both shares in one guarded call. Routed through either claim
  function, it would refuse itself by name.
- **The three already debited before they paid, and paid through the vault's lock.**
  - Each debit comes before `_release`: `p.creatorPaid += amount` (`:1489`),
    `_referrerPending[poolId][referrer] -= amount` (`:1507`) and `p.platformUsed += amount`
    (`:1685`).
  - `_release` pays only inside `vault.lock` (`:1652`), where `lockAcquired` burns the hook's claim
    and takes the token to the payee (`:1456-1457`).
  - Until v1.6.28 that was the whole protection. A nested call found nothing left to pay, and a
    nested payout was refused `LockerAlreadySet(address)` by the vault. Both remain underneath the
    guard, and the red run below shows each at work on `24d21c2`. The debit's order stays pinned on
    its own (`FeastPlatformExit`, below).
- **Nothing else moves.** `claim` still asks the guardian first (`_requireUnfrozen()`, `:1547`), so
  the freeze table is unchanged. Modifiers are not part of the ABI, which is unchanged, and no entry
  point is added or removed. `check-boundaries` `surface`, `abi` and `guarded` are green on this
  head.
- **Never on `lockAcquired` or the venue's callbacks**, as in v1.6.24. The venue calls
  `lockAcquired` from inside each payout's own lock.

**What the tests show.**

- **From inside a conversion.** This uses `I7_GuardedEntryPoints`'s existing harness: a hostile route
  pool re-enters from inside a creator's `claimAsBnb`. Each of the three is refused by the guard, by
  name. On `24d21c2` each was entered:
  - `claimCreator` found the share already debited;
  - `claimReferrer` paid the referrer from inside the creator's conversion;
  - `releasePlatformFees` paid the platform's share. Its test runs after genesis, so the sink is
    bound and a re-entry the guard let through would pay rather than be refused
    `PlatformSinkNotBound`.
- **From inside its own payout.** The quote token re-enters during the vault's transfer to the payee
  and records the revert data. Each of the three is refused by name and pays exactly once.
- **One flag for all.** From inside `claimCreator`'s payout, `claimReferrer`, `releasePlatformFees`
  and `burnFilledCoin` are all refused by name. On `24d21c2` the first two met the vault's
  `LockerAlreadySet` (`0xc6742a3f`), and `burnFilledCoin` was entered.
- **The flag comes down after each call.** In one transaction, through a batching contract (under
  `isolate = true` each top-level call is its own transaction), the three run one after another. Each
  refuses a re-entry while it pays and clears the flag on the way out, and `burnFilledCoin` runs
  after the last of them.

## The one behaviour change

**What changes.** A quote token that calls `releasePlatformFees`, `claimCreator` or `claimReferrer`
from inside its own transfer, that is, from inside one of the hook's payouts, now meets
`ReentrancyGuardReentrantCall()`. Until v1.6.28 that call was entered, found the share already
debited, and returned, and the payout succeeded with no extra payment.

- **If the token lets the refusal through**, its transfer reverts and the payout reverts with it.
  The revert is the vault's ERC-7751 wrap:
  `WrappedError(quote, transfer.selector, ReentrancyGuardReentrantCall(), ERC20TransferFailed())`.
  Every entitlement stays where it was: stuck, never lost, as under a paused quote.
- **If the token swallows the refusal**, the payout completes exactly as before.
- Either way nothing is paid twice.

**Who it affects.** Only the pool quoted in that token. A pool's payouts move only its own quote, and
a second pool quoted in an ordinary token pays as before.

**Not a new kind of refusal.** It is the same refusal, from the same flag, that `claimAsBnb`,
`buyBackAndBurn`, `burnFilledCoin` and `collectLpFees` have given a re-entrant call since v1.6.24, and
that `claim` gave until v1.6.28. The same refusal now also meets a call into these three from inside
any other guarded call, such as a `claimAsBnb` conversion, where until v1.6.28 it was entered (above).

**Where it is pinned.** `test_aQuoteThatLetsTheRefusalThroughHoldsUpOnlyItsOwnPoolsPayouts`
(`I7_PayoutsHoldTheGuard.t.sol:243`) covers each of the three functions: the wrapped revert asserted
byte for byte, every entitlement intact, and the second pool paying. The swallowing case is every
own-payout test above. On `24d21c2`'s source that test fails "next call did not revert as expected":
the payout went through.

## The three tests the move turned red, resolved

The full suite on `2002ad5` failed exactly three existing tests, all outside the lane's first card
(`src/FeastHook.sol`, `test/audit/astrasec/`, this note). The coordinator's ruling of 7 October
(option A) widened the card to the four files below. Each was changed as proposed and verified, with

   - `test_theHookIsTheV1628Hook` (`:419`) replaces `test_theHookIsByteIdenticalToV1626`, whose

   - The other seventeen pins are unchanged, and hold.
2. **`FeastPlatformExit.test_aQuoteThatReentersTheExitCannotDoubleThePayout`** (`5add940`,
   `:195-227`).
   - Round 2 (L2) required the nested `releasePlatformFees`, called back by the quote from inside
     the exit's own payout, to *succeed* and pay nothing. That was how it saw the debit come before
     the payout.
   - With the exit guarded, the nested call is refused whatever the order, so the witness now pins
     two facts. First, the nested exit reverts `ReentrancyGuardReentrantCall()`, asserted by name: a
     probe on the test contract (`:235-238`), called by the quote from inside the payout, calls the
     exit again and keeps the revert data the token discards. Second, the debit precedes the payout:
     the same probe reads `platformPending`, a view no guard covers, from inside the payout, and
     requires 0.
   - **The addition.** The proposal asserted only that the nested exit failed. The ruling asked for
     the revert by name, so the probe re-enters too.
   - **Measured in a minimal tree.** The witness is green on this head, and dies three ways: with the
     platform debit moved after the payout, on "the bucket was already debited when the token moved:
     1980198019801980198 != 0"; with `releasePlatformFees` unguarded, on "the nested exit is
     refused"; and with the guard reverting `0x3ee5aeb6` instead, on "by the guard, by name".
3. **`docs/site-census.md`** (`d204d5c`).
   - `releasePlatformFees`'s reentrancy row (`:399`) is restated.
   - Rows for `claimCreator` and `claimReferrer` are added (`:400-401`).
   - The declared count moves 261 → 263, by those two rows only.
4. **`docs/interface.md:372-379`** (`d204d5c`). The guarded set is now the seven, and `claim` refuses
   one call down.
